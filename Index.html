Lesson 1 · The gas turbine

Air in, power out

A gas turbine runs the Brayton cycle: it compresses air, burns fuel in it at constant pressure, then expands the hot gas through a turbine. Three core sections do the work: compressor, combustion and turbine.

  • For inspection, the engine splits into a cold section (inlet, compressor, diffuser) and a hot section (combustor, turbine, exhaust).
  • The hot section's typical damage is cracking, from constant heating and cooling.
  • Turbine inlet temperature is the most critical variable. It sets the engine's limits.
  • Standard sea level (ISA): 29.92 inHg and 15 °C.

Lesson 2 · Ducts and nozzles

Squeeze it, it speeds up

Inside the engine, air is below the speed of sound. At those speeds the duct shape decides what happens. The same mass of air must pass every cross-section each second.

  • Convergent (narrowing): velocity increases, pressure decreases. Examples: nozzle guide vanes, the exhaust nozzle.
  • Divergent (widening): velocity decreases, pressure increases. Examples: the diffuser, the passages between stator vanes.
  • Gas reaches its highest velocity in the turbine section, where the nozzle guide vanes accelerate it.

Lesson 3 · Compressors

Stage by stage

An axial compressor is rows of spinning rotor blades with rows of fixed stator vanes between them. One rotor row plus one stator row is a stage.

  • Rotors add energy. Stators slow the air, turn velocity into pressure (a diffuser action) and direct it into the next rotor at the right angle. The last row, at the discharge, straightens the flow to remove turbulence.
  • The number of stages sets the pressure ratio. A dual axial (two-spool) design gets a higher compression ratio.
  • At constant RPM, blade angle of attack changes with the velocity of the incoming air. At low RPM a bleed air valve dumps air to prevent stall.
  • The diffuser, between the compressor and the burner, raises pressure and lowers velocity. Highest pressure in the engine is at the compressor outlet.
  • Dirty blades lose efficiency and raise EGT. Washing them on the wing is field cleaning. Severe rubbing causes galling and cracking.

Axial flow

  • High compression ratio (many stages)
  • High peak efficiency
  • Small frontal area
  • More fragile, needs many parts

Centrifugal flow

  • Two elements: impeller and diffuser
  • High pressure rise per stage
  • Rugged and simple
  • Turbine shaft joins it by a splined coupling
Lesson 4 · Combustion

Burn a quarter, cool with the rest

The combustion section mixes fuel with air and burns it at constant pressure. Air passing through it both supports combustion and cools the engine.

  • About a quarter of the air is primary air that feeds the flame. The rest is secondary air: it cools the liner walls with a film and dilutes the gas before the turbine.
  • Three types are used: multiple-can, annular and can-annular.
  • In a duplex fuel nozzle, the flow divider opens the main orifice when fuel pressure rises.
  • Hot spots in the burner point to malfunctioning fuel nozzles. Hot spots on the tail cone point to a nozzle or a faulty combustion chamber.

Lesson 5 · The turbine

Where the heat does the work

Each turbine stage has two elements: a stator (the nozzle guide vanes) and a rotor (the bladed wheel). The vanes form convergent passages that speed the gas up and aim it at the blades at the right angle.

  • The first-stage inlet guide vanes get the highest heat-to-metal contact in the engine.
  • Most blades are reaction-impulse: impulse at the root, reaction at the tip. Blades are held by a fir-tree root.
  • Shrouded blades have tips that join into a band. This reduces vibration and improves airflow.
  • Some engines put more than one wheel on a shaft to extract more power than one wheel can.
  • Blades removed for inspection go back in the same slot, because the set is balanced together.

Lesson 6 · Starting and shutdown

Starter, ignition, fuel

The proper sequence is starter, ignition, fuel. The starter gives airflow first, the igniters must be firing before fuel arrives, and fuel goes in last.

  • The first sign of a good start is a rise in EGT (light-off). Watch TIT/EGT to catch a hot start.
  • Hot start: EGT goes above its limit. Hung start: the engine lights but fails to reach idle RPM.
  • Keep the starter engaged until the engine is self-sustaining at idle RPM.
  • Fire during start: turn the fuel off and keep cranking to blow the fire out.
  • Before shutdown, a cool-off period lets the turbine wheel cool before the thinner case contracts around it.

Lesson 7 · Engine instruments

Reading the engine

  • The tachometer is a primary engine instrument, calibrated in percent RPM.
  • Helicopters need two tachometer indications: engine RPM and main rotor RPM.
  • Engine temperatures are measured with chromel-alumel thermocouples. EGT gives a relative indication of turbine inlet temperature and shows the turbine's mechanical health.
  • EPR = turbine outlet total pressure ÷ engine inlet total pressure. Turning on anti-icing at a fixed power lever lowers EPR, because bleed air is taken from the cycle.

Lesson 8 · Engine systems

Fuel, oil and fire

Figure 10 · Fuel path
  1. Tank boost pump

    Gives a positive flow of fuel to the engine pump.

  2. Engine-driven pump

    Raises fuel to working pressure.

  3. Shutoff valve

    Usually downstream of the engine-driven pump.

  4. Fuel control unit

    Meters fuel from compressor outlet pressure, turbine temperature and RPM. Re-rig the engine after replacing it.

  5. Flow divider and duplex nozzles

    Fuel pressure opens the main orifice.

Where fuel lines can't be kept apart from wiring, run them below the wiring and clamp them to structure.

Oil

  • The temperature gauge reads oil entering the engine.
  • The oil pressure regulator is adjusted to set system pressure.
  • Synthetic oil evaporates less at high temperature and forms less lacquer and coke.
  • Oil is changed on schedule because it gets contaminated with fine solid particles.

Fire and ice

  • A continuous-loop detector is an overheat detector.
  • Extinguishers fire by an electrically discharged cartridge (squib).
  • Anti-icing ducts engine bleed air through the inlet's critical areas.
  • Most exhaust failures are thermal-fatigue cracks from improper welding in manufacture.
Lesson 9 · Twin-Pac and the rules

Helicopter specifics

  • The Twin-Pac has two power sections, each with its own turbine, driving one combining gearbox (C-box). Each power section has its own ignition system. There are three oil systems: one per power section plus the C-box.
  • On a governed engine, raising or lowering the collective has no effect on the twist-grip throttle position. A friction control holds the grip at the selected position.
Figure 11 · Twin-Pac layout

Paperwork

  • An AD is complied with as specified in the AD.
  • Time and cycle limits for an installed engine are in the aircraft manufacturer's Instructions for Continued Airworthiness.

Inspection habits

  • Never mark hot-section parts with a lead pencil. The graphite can cause cracks.
  • Reinstall turbine blades in the same slot.
  • After replacing a fuel control, re-rig the engine.

About the answer keys

The four exams share many questions. Repeats were merged, and spelling was cleaned up. In a few places the keys disagree with each other or with turbine theory. This course uses the answer below and shows a note when the question comes up.

  • Fuel shutoff valve: Model 2 marks “before the driven fuel pump”. Models 1 and 3 say downstream of the engine-driven pump.
  • Fire extinguisher activation: Model 2 marks “piston stem and plunger”. Used here: electrically discharged cartridge.
  • AD compliance: Model 2 marks “next scheduled inspection”. Used here: as specified in the AD.
  • Starter disengagement: Model 4 marks “ignition and fuel system are activated”. Used here: engine reaches idle RPM.
  • Highest gas velocity: Model 4 marks “diffuser”. A diffuser slows air down, so this course uses turbine.
  • Model 3's “two basic sections in turbine engine” was left out. Its key (combustion chamber and turbine) matches no standard answer.
